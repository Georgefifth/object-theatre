# Playtest iteration: a rescue with consequences

## Independent baseline
An independent agent actually played the previous horror audition in a separate local browser: summon Mouse, three linear beats, then Mouse disappears and every audition gets the same approval. The playable complication was resolved by the author instead of the visitor.
The proposed rescue initially used wide stage-bottom buttons. The reviewer correctly challenged this as moving choices rather than adding agency, and flagged the disconnected signature scene and incomplete accessibility descriptions.

## Changes
Mouse now drags the stage away. Graphical stage actions let the player pull its USB cable or grab it. Grabbing fails visibly and requires a repair using Cup or Key. There are three outcomes:
- Unplug: stage stops; lights and enabled soundtrack go off through act one.
- Cup: the original illustrated Cup flips upside down as Mouse’s rental home, instead of standing with the cast.
- Key: the original Key becomes a wheel brake instead of standing with the cast.
Consequences persist into the following scenes, alter the signature/IOU choice, explain Eraser’s debt collection and button takeover, resolve at the snack interval, and get specific callbacks in act three. No timer, reflex penalty or game-over loop is added. All controls are accessible native buttons, not a drag simulation.
Older saves that already passed the old horror scene remain valid and are not assigned an invented rescue decision.

## Verification and limits
The primary agent actually operated all three rescue outcomes in the browser, checked the persistent Cup effect at verdict, and tested graphical action bounds at 320×568. Meaningful tests cover stage action dispatch, failed grab/repair, consequences, history rollback, old save compatibility, scene descriptions and blackout audio/resume. The story graph has 91 bilingual nodes and 5,120 route combinations; 161 sampled routes render and switch languages successfully. Combinations do not represent unique authored stories.
The independent reviewer inspected actual Cup-result and verdict screenshots, confirmed the visible consequence, and found the next signature scene disconnected. That feedback drove the signature/IOU rewrite. Its browser connection failed during the new-version retest, so this is not an independent full replay of the revised game.
Only the horror audition gains this small interactive incident. CEO/court and much of the remaining story retain linear dialogue. No claim is made that enjoyment or retention improved; an agent review is not evidence of real-player preference.
